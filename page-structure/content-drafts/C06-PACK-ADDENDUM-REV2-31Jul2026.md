# ==== labor-colony ====

## Labour Colony, Built as a Site, Delivered as Buildings

A labour colony from SAMAN is worker housing planned as a whole site and delivered as finished steel buildings. The dormitories on this page come out of our Bengaluru and Greater Noida factories as newly fabricated structures, and around them the rest of a working colony takes shape: toilet and bath blocks matched to headcount, kitchen and dining space, drinking water points, gate cabins and internal lighting. Across 500+ projects in 15+ states we have learned where colonies actually fail, and it is rarely the steel. It is drainage that cannot take a monsoon, stairs that jam at shift change, boards mounted where no electrician can reach. This page exists so none of that reaches your site.

[IMAGE labor-colony-120x24-gplus1-front-right-hero-wide.webp | alt: Two-floor labour colony dormitory of 120x24 ft seen across the yard, stair access and walkway rails along the full front.]

## What Counts as a Labour Colony?

The term covers any organised residential setup for site workers, from one dormitory beside a small project to a multi-building campus for a thousand-man workforce. On this page it means the campus version: several buildings, shared sanitation and cooking, planned circulation and security. One dormitory alone is better served by the specific building types below.

## Three Building Types, One Decision

Choosing between the cluster's buildings is the real decision on this page. Where bed count per rupee decides, the [open-hall dormitory building](/product/labor-colony/labor-sheds) houses the most workers under one supervised roof. Where gangs need lockable privacy for long postings, [room-based hutment blocks](/product/labor-colony/labor-hutments) split every floor into separate rooms. And where the workforce follows the work, [camp blocks built to relocate](/product/labor-colony/prefab-labor-camps) come down at one site and stand again at the next.

# ==== labor-sheds ====

## The Labour Shed: Maximum Beds, One Open Hall

The labour shed is built around a single idea: house the most workers in one supervised space for the least money. Every floor is one clear hall with no internal walls, facing bed rows split by an aisle that runs end to end. A light portal frame keeps the span open, PPGI cladding keeps weather out, and ventilator banks up high keep air crossing the hall without fans fighting the heat alone.

[IMAGE labor-sheds-120x24-gplus1-front-right-hero-wide.webp | alt: Open-hall labour shed of 120x24 ft from the corner, gable roof over two sleeping floors.]

## Why an Open Hall Works

Because the hall is one space, running it is simple in ways room blocks cannot match. From the doorway one supervisor counts every bed. Cleaning is one crew, one pass. Moving beds between crews is a list change, not a room change. Exits, emergency lighting and zoned circuits are engineered for open-hall occupancy from the first drawing, which is what separates a compliant shed from a shed-shaped problem.

Workers keep belongings behind a lock, gangs keep to their own rooms, and supervisors stop refereeing the disputes open halls generate.

## Description-tab content, one per page, links inline as marked

### labor-colony

A working labour colony is more than a row of dormitories, and this page is planned that way. The buildings above are newly fabricated steel structures from our Bengaluru and Greater Noida factories, and around them a complete site takes shape: toilet and bath blocks matched to headcount, kitchen and dining space, drinking water points, gate cabins and lighting along the internal roads. SAMAN has delivered worker housing within 500+ projects across 15+ states, and that experience shows in details contractors notice the second time: drainage falls that survive monsoon, stair widths that clear a shift change, distribution boards an electrician can actually reach.

Three building types carry the cluster, and choosing between them is the real decision. Where bed count per rupee decides, the [open-hall dormitory building](/product/labor-colony/labor-sheds) packs the highest headcount into a single supervised hall. Where gangs need lockable privacy for long postings, [room-based hutment blocks](/product/labor-colony/labor-hutments) split every floor into separate rooms. And where the workforce follows the work, [camp blocks built to relocate](/product/labor-colony/prefab-labor-camps) come down at one site and stand again at the next.

Every building follows the same commercial rules. Prices are ex-factory against a flat rate per square foot, so a bigger building never hides a bigger margin. Your fixed quotation reaches you inside 48 hours, dispatch follows 7 to 21 working days behind drawing approval, and structures carry the 5-year structural warranty and 1-year finishing warranty as standard. Send your plot dimensions with the enquiry and the returned layout places buildings, services and access on your actual plot rather than a generic rectangle.

### labor-sheds

The labour shed is built around one idea: maximum bunks under one supervised roof at minimum cost. Each floor is a single clear hall, no internal walls, with facing bed rows split by an aisle that runs end to end. A light portal-frame structure keeps the span open, PPGI cladding keeps the weather out, and continuous ventilator banks up high keep air crossing the hall without fans fighting the heat load alone.

Because the hall is one space, everything about running it is simpler. From the doorway one supervisor counts every bed. Cleaning is one crew and one pass. Re-allocating beds between crews is a list change, not a room change. Fire exits, emergency lighting and zoned circuits are engineered for open-hall occupancy from the first drawing, which is what separates a compliant shed from a shed-shaped problem.

The building arrives as fabricated components and stands in days on a prepared base, with an RCC slab preferred at high occupancies. Zoned lighting lets night-shift workers sleep while the day shift dresses. If your site needs lockable rooms rather than a hall, [the hutment room block](/product/labor-colony/labor-hutments) serves that brief; if the housing must move between projects, [the relocatable camp build](/product/labor-colony/prefab-labor-camps) does. And to weigh all three types on one page, [the worker housing range](/product/labor-colony) compares them side by side. This page is for the contractor whose priority is simply beds, delivered fast, at the lowest cost per worker housed.

### labor-hutments

A hutment block trades a little capacity for a lot of order. Instead of one open hall, each floor is divided into individual sleeping rooms along a passage, each room having a lockable door, sliding aluminium window, fan points and its own small board. Workers keep belongings behind a lock, gangs keep to their own rooms, and supervisors stop refereeing the disputes that open halls generate.

The structure is MS framing, welded or bolted to suit the site, behind an insulated corrugated skin, floors of board on steel joists, and services run room by room so a fault in one room never darkens the block. The passage carries its own night circuit. Rooms repeat identically floor to floor, which makes allocation by gang, trade or shift a paper exercise rather than a negotiation.

Hutments earn their keep on longer postings. When a crew lives on site for seasons, room privacy stops being a luxury and starts being why workers stay, and several principal contractors now write room-based accommodation into subcontract audit standards. The rate runs slightly above [the open-hall shed building](/product/labor-colony/labor-sheds) for the same area, and the bed count per floor is lower; that is the deliberate trade. Crews that relocate between projects should weigh [the demountable camp block](/product/labor-colony/prefab-labor-camps) instead, and [the colony range overview](/product/labor-colony) sets all three types against each other. Pick the room count from the six sizes below, and the quotation returns with the room schedule and passage plan drawn for that configuration.

### prefab-labor-camps

Most worker housing is written off when the project ends. The prefab labour camp is engineered so it is not: wall, floor and roof panels share one bolted module, the whole block stands on pedestal pads instead of a poured slab, and electrical blocks plug together through external cable trays. When the project finishes, the camp unbolts, travels on trailers, panels numbered for reassembly, and stands again at the next site far faster than it first went up.

That mobility changes the arithmetic. Rate for rate a camp runs a little above [a fixed open-hall shed](/product/labor-colony/labor-sheds) of the same size, and repays the difference the first time it moves. Across three projects the same block becomes the cheapest housing a contractor owns. Pedestal footings also mean leaving a site means lifting pads, never breaking concrete, so ground reinstatement takes days and deposits come back.

The blocks group along a single services spine carrying ring-main water and zoned power, and repeat customers carry one block numbering from site to site so the layout plan follows the buildings with the buildings. Crews wanting lockable rooms in a movable block should compare [the room-block hutment](/product/labor-colony/labor-hutments) first. If your work is one long project on one site, [the full colony line-up](/product/labor-colony) shows the fixed buildings that cost less for the same area. If your projects move, this is the building that moves with them.

## BLOCK 11 - PRODUCT DETAILS: DESCRIPTION TAB

### What counts as a shipping container home on this page

Search for shipping container homes in India and the results split three ways. Some pages show American and Australian architectural projects with two-storey stacks and cantilevers. Some show new-built prefab cabins that borrow container proportions but were never freight boxes. A third group sells the thing this page sells: a genuine cargo shell, converted for residential use, delivered to an Indian site.

SAMAN builds in both of the last two categories, and keeping them apart is the point of this page. A shipping container home here means a residential conversion of a documented freight or cargo container. The bottom rails, corner posts, ISO corner castings, corrugated side walls and steel roof of the donor box are retained wherever they are sound, and the conversion works around them. A prefab container home, by contrast, is built from new steel to the same proportions with no donor-condition variable at all. Both give you the same room programme in the same six sizes. Only one of them gives you a real container, and only one of them requires a condition assessment before work starts.

The distinction is not cosmetic. It changes what has to be checked, what can be promised, and what the drawing has to show. If your reason for wanting a container home is the look, either page will get you there. If your reason is that you want an actual shipping container, this is the page.

It also changes what the six published sizes mean. A 20 ft plan here is a single donor box, converted whole. A 40 ft plan is either one 40 ft donor or two boxes joined on a drawn connection. Widths above the standard 8 ft are widened conversions rather than stock ISO boxes, which is why the 10 ft and 12 ft plans carry more connection steel and a different transport conversation than the 8 ft ones. None of that is visible in a render, and all of it is on the drawing.

### How a cargo shell becomes a house you can live in

The sequence is fixed. The donor box is received and its condition is recorded: dents, corrosion, previous cargo, patch repairs and coating history. The original cargo flooring is checked for damage, contamination and treatment history, because marine plywood that carried unknown freight is not a clean residential substrate until somebody has established that it is. Cross-members and bottom rails that are corroded or have been cut in a previous life are repaired before anything is built on top of them.

Only then does cutting begin. Every opening is set out from the approved conversion drawing, cut, and immediately framed. Local steel is added below wet rooms, under large openings and along joined-module edges. The floor is either retained with a documented acceptance, or replaced or overlaid with sealed 18 to 19 mm structural board on the repaired cross-members, which is also what lets the wet-area waterproofing be turned up properly at the corrugations.

The envelope goes on next. Inside the corrugated shell an independent service wall is built, so wiring and plumbing stay accessible and condensation is controlled without hiding the steel behind them. Nominal 50 mm PUF, PIR, closed-cell or mineral-wool insulation wraps corrugation valleys, posts and opening frames, with continuous air and vapour control chosen for the climate and the fire strategy. A loose blanket pressed against bare steel is not accepted in a humid residential conversion, because that is exactly where interstitial condensation collects.

The roof is treated as its own system. The original steel deck is inspected, repaired and protected, and an insulated inner ceiling, typically over a 75 to 100 mm build-up, is added below it. Where solar gain or rain noise is severe a ventilated secondary over-roof can be added. Ceiling voids then have to coordinate roof bows, wiring, exhaust runs, air-conditioner drains and, on joined plans, the joint beams, which is why the clear height is quoted after the insulation and ceiling build-up rather than from the raw box.

### What cutting an opening does to a container wall

A corrugated container side wall is a shear panel. It is carrying racking load, and the corrugation profile is what gives it that stiffness. Cut a 4 ft window into it and some of that capacity leaves with the steel. Cut a sliding door, and more does. Remove a length of side wall to join two boxes, and the panel stops being a panel at all.

That is why every opening on a SAMAN conversion gets a welded subframe with a header, a sill and jamb reinforcement tied back to the primary structure, and why large glazing and inter-container openings get designed portal frames rather than a trimmed hole. It is also why the conversion drawing records temporary bracing: a box distorts while it is being cut, and if it distorts before the frame is in, the doors never close properly again.

The practical consequence for a buyer is simple. Unreinforced site cutting after delivery is prohibited on these units. If you want another window, it belongs on the drawing before fabrication, not in a conversation with a local fabricator six months later. The same applies to anything you plan to hang off the roof. Water tanks, deck frames, solar structures and canopies do not sit on thin cargo roof sheet. They transfer to verified rails, posts or added steel, with independent waterproofing, and that is an engineering item, not an accessory.

### Heat, condensation and rain noise inside a steel box

A converted container is close to airtight and highly conductive, which is a useful combination for air-conditioning and a difficult one for moisture. Three things have to be designed together.

Ventilation comes first. Openable cross-ventilation plus kitchen and bathroom extract is the baseline, and controlled fresh air is added where occupancy, cooking or sealed glazing needs it. Extract discharge, air-conditioner condensate and fresh-air inlets are routed away from roof valleys and container joints, because those are the two places where water finds its way back in.

Condensation risk is then assessed at the steel bridges and closed cavities, which is where it actually happens: at corner posts, at opening frames, and behind cavity closures. The insulation is detailed to reduce those bridges without blocking drainage or making the steel impossible to inspect later.

Rain noise is the third, and it is the one people forget until the first monsoon. A bare cargo roof under heavy rain in Mangaluru or Guwahati is loud. The insulated ceiling build-up is what brings it down, and it works as a system with the roof repair and the vapour layer rather than as a separate acoustic product. Insulation is not compressed around roof bows, light fittings or module joins to make it fit, because a compressed section is both a thermal bridge and an acoustic gap, and the roof repairs underneath still have to stay inspectable years later.

Every price on this page is calculated the same way. SAMAN's controlled base rate for Container Houses is Rs 1,600 per sq.ft at a 200 sq.ft reference. The published figure is a conversion price for a documented shipping or cargo shell with engineered openings, insulation, residential lining and fixed services. ### Foundations, transport and what to check on arrival

A converted container is a point-loaded structure. It bears on its corner castings and on designated support points, not on a continuous slab edge, and the support points, anchor locations and allowable bearing are shown on the signed general arrangement drawing. Getting this wrong is the most common site failure: a unit set on an uneven plinth will rack, and a racked box shows it first in the doors and the window frames.

Transport status is the second thing to settle early. A CSC plate or cargo certification held by the donor box before conversion does not survive the conversion. Cutting openings, joining boxes, adding stairs or stacking changes the load path, and none of it is a transport approval for the completed residential unit unless it has been separately engineered and documented. At 8 ft width the movement conversation is routine. At 10 and 12 ft it is not, and it belongs in the order rather than in the week before dispatch.

On arrival, the pre-dispatch dossier is what you check against. It identifies the approved drawings, the material schedule, test results, photographs, punch items, lifting data and the site connection points. Lift and transport only to the approved method statement, rigging geometry and support-point drawing, with interior fit-out and wet tanks secured. Forklift handling is permitted only where pockets and capacity are documented.

Exposure is the third thing worth settling at order rather than after. The coating schedule records preparation, product, colour, coat count or dry-film target, cure and touch-up, and it is upgraded for coastal, industrial or high-moisture sites. On a converted container the vulnerable places are not the flat panels but the cut edges: every opening, every removed section and every service penetration is a fresh edge, and an edge coated once at the factory in a specification meant for an inland site will not last on a Konkan or Gujarat coastal plot. Tell SAMAN the exposure before the coating is specified, not when the first rust bloom appears.

### Warranty, service life and the maintenance needed

Structural warranty on a SAMAN conversion is 10 years. Finishing warranty is 1 year, extendable to 2. Lead time is 7 to 21 working days from a confirmed order and approved drawings. SAMAN is ISO 9001:2015 certified and has completed over 500 projects.

Warranty does not cover unapproved alteration, poor foundations, site water entry, utility faults, corrosive exposure beyond the specified coating system, missed seal and coating maintenance, misuse, or relocation damage. That list reads like boilerplate, but on a converted container two of those items do most of the damage: site water entry through a penetration somebody added later, and missed coating maintenance at a cut edge.

Maintenance itself is short. Keep the roof drainage and the flashings at any module joint clear and inspectable. Touch up coating at cut edges, especially in coastal and industrial exposure, where the coating system is upgraded at order rather than repaired later. Keep the service wall accessible so a plumbing or wiring fault does not become a demolition job. Nothing here is unusual for a steel building; all of it is easier if the drawing is followed.

### Questions buyers ask before ordering a conversion

**Is this made from an actual shipping container?**
Yes. The donor is a documented freight or cargo container, and its rails, corner posts, corrugated walls and roof are retained wherever they are sound. If you want the same plan in new-built steel, that is the prefab container homes page instead.

**Is a container house legal in India?**
It depends on your local authority, not on the unit. Municipal, fire and structural approvals for the completed residential configuration are confirmed project by project and sit outside the published price. SAMAN supplies the drawings and evidence the approval needs.

**How much does a container house cost?**
On this page, from Rs 2,81,600 ex-GST for 160 sq.ft to Rs 7,21,920 ex-GST for 480 sq.ft, calculated from a Rs 1,600 per sq.ft base at 200 sq.ft with fixed area-band adjustments. Foundations, transport and approvals are separate.

**Can I add a window after delivery?**
No. Unreinforced site cutting is prohibited on these units, because a corrugated side wall is carrying shear. Extra openings belong on the conversion drawing before fabrication so the portal frame is built in.

**Can it be relocated later?**
It can be moved, but the transport status of the converted unit is established for the completed configuration and is not inherited from the donor box's cargo certification. Relocation damage is outside warranty unless the move follows the approved method statement.

**Does the container get hot inside?**
Not with the envelope described above. The wall build-up is nominally 50 mm PUF or PIR with continuous vapour control, and the roof carries a 75 to 100 mm insulated build-up that also handles rain noise. What matters as much is ventilation and condensate routing, which are designed with it.

**What is the delivery time?**
7 to 21 working days from a confirmed order with approved drawings. Foundations and site readiness are yours; delivery is scheduled against them.

**Can two containers be joined?**
Yes, above 20 ft the plan is delivered either as one 40 ft box or as joined modules. The connection frames, waste fall across the joint and flexible service connections are drawn beforehand, because differential movement is what breaks concealed pipework.

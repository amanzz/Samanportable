Detailed information about Portable Mobile Laboratory

Portable mobile laboratory, or a laboratory on wheels? Read this first

Search for a mobile laboratory in India and most of what comes back is a vehicle. A lab built into a truck body, a diagnostic unit on a motorbike, a science van that visits schools. Those are real products and they solve a real problem, which is testing that has to move from place to place during the working week. This page is about the other thing that shares the name.

A SAMAN portable mobile laboratory is a factory-built steel room. It is finished as a laboratory in the works, loaded onto a trailer, lifted off by its four top-corner eyes and set down on prepared supports. It is portable in the sense that it can be moved when the project moves, or relocated between plants, not in the sense that it drives to a sampling point and comes back. If your testing has to happen at a different address every day, a vehicle is the right answer and nothing here will help you.

If instead your testing happens in one place for months or years, and that place has no building fit to hold it, a placed laboratory is usually the cheaper and more workable answer. It gives you a full 8.5 ft standing height, benching along the walls at working depth, mains power through a proper distribution board, and a floor and wall finish designed to be washed. A vehicle body gives you none of those at the same cost.

The word cabin also appears on other parts of this site and it is worth separating them now. A container office is built from or like an ISO shipping container and belongs on the Container Offices pages. An office cabin is a dry office interior with desks, and belongs on the Portable Office pages. The laboratory sits apart from both, because the thing that defines it is not its shape but its finish: the room is built to be cleaned.

Who orders a portable mobile laboratory, and where it stands

The enquiries that end in an order share one shape. There is a process running at a fixed location, that process produces samples, and the samples currently travel somewhere else to be tested. Every hour of that travel is an hour the plant runs on an assumption. The buyer wants the test next to the process.

In practice that means a quality control room at a ready-mix or aggregate plant, taking cube and slump samples off the batching line. A soil and material testing room on a road or rail package, where the laboratory follows the works. A sample receiving and screening point at a food or agricultural processing unit. A works laboratory at a chemical or pharmaceutical site where an existing building has no spare room that can be brought to a cleanable standard. A metering and calibration room beside a utility installation.

The people using it are a lab technician, a sample handler, a quality engineer and, periodically, a maintenance engineer who needs to reach the services without dismantling the benching. The unit is normally placed in a testing compound or beside a sample receiving point, on a levelled base, with a wash-down approach and a utility trench or route already agreed. Where it stands is a decision that has to be made before the drawing is frozen, because the sealed service sleeve is cut on one wall at one coordinate, and that coordinate has to face the services.

What lab-ready means on this page, and what it does not

Lab-ready here means the enclosure and the fixed provisions scheduled on the approved GA drawing, and nothing beyond them. That is a deliberately narrow definition and it is the honest one, because the difference between a quoted laboratory enclosure and a working accredited laboratory can be several times the price of the room.

What the price on this page covers is the steel shell in one of the six approved sizes at 8.5 ft nominal height on 100 mm walls; the coved vinyl floor finish, which turns the floor-to-wall junction into a radius rather than a corner; the washable wall liner with cleanable junctions; the non-absorbent bench substrate; the fixed bench runs, the single sink or service interface and the upper storage modules exactly as the GA schedules them in feet; the door and window schedule with end walls kept furniture-free; the standard electrical package with distribution board, RCCB and MCB protection, earthing and circuit segregation; one sealed utility sleeve; and four compact top-corner lifting eyes.

What it does not cover, and what is quoted separately or excluded outright:

  • Instruments and loose equipment of every kind. The balances, microscopes and analysers in the photographs are customer-supplied and are shown to give the benching scale.
  • Fume hoods and any process exhaust system. The drawing holds a cowl position; the cowl, duct, fan and any containment are not supplied.
  • Laboratory gases, gas lines, cylinders and manifolds.
  • RO or DI water plant, storage and distribution.
  • Waste and effluent treatment of any kind. The sink is a water and waste interface, not a treatment system.
  • Validation, qualification and accreditation of the laboratory or of any test carried out in it.
  • Site water supply, site drainage, the incoming electrical supply and the licensed connection work.
  • Delivery, unloading, placement and site supports, which are covered by the Shipping tab and the quotation.
  • Fitted air conditioning. The electrical package carries provision where the drawing calls for it; the unit itself is quoted separately.

If a requirement is not in the first list and not in the second, it has not been priced and it should be raised before the drawing is frozen rather than after. Final structural member adequacy, lifting connections and capacity, wind and seismic actions, equipment loads, the final electrical load schedule, derating and voltage drop all remain project engineering, controlled by the signed quotation and the approved fabrication drawings.

Why the cleaning aisle decides the width

Every GA board on this page prints one figure that a floor-area comparison will never show you: the clear cleaning aisle. It is the width of floor left between the bench faces once the benching is in, and it is the number that governs how the room actually works.

At 5.34 ft, which is what the 10 ft widths give once both long walls are benched, one person works comfortably and a second person passes behind them. A trolley passes, but the person at the bench steps in. At 7.34 ft, which the 10×10 gives with one bench and the 20×12 gives with three, a trolley and a working technician coexist without either stopping. Neither figure is generous by fixed-laboratory standards, and both are stated so that nobody discovers the difference after delivery.

This is also why none of the six sizes carries an internal partition, and why the 20×12 has no island bench even though it is wide enough to hold one. A partition in a 10 or 12 ft width produces a corner that cannot be reached properly with a mop, and an island produces four more of them. The coved floor junction exists to remove exactly those corners. Adding a partition back in would undo the reason the room costs what it costs.

The end walls follow the same logic. Each carries one centred 4 by 3 ft window and no external door, and each is kept furniture-free. That is not a saving; it is what keeps two of the four wall-floor junctions completely clear for cleaning and inspection.

How the price is built, and what is quoted separately

The price on this page is built from one editable rate and one published area rule, not from a quotation for each size. The base rate is ₹2,000 per square foot at 200 square feet, which is why the 20×10 is the reference size. Smaller units carry a band uplift because the fixed content of a laboratory does not shrink with the floor: a 100 sq ft room still needs a sink, a distribution board, a sealed sleeve and a coved floor. Larger units carry a band reduction.

The bands applied on this page are +15 per cent between 100 and 149 sq ft, +10 per cent between 150 and 199, no adjustment at 200, minus 4 per cent between 201 and 300, and minus 5 per cent between 301 and 400. That is how ₹2,000 becomes ₹2,300 per sq ft at the 10×10 and ₹1,900 per sq ft at the 40×10. The full ladder runs from 40 sq ft up to 900 sq ft; above 900 sq ft there is no rule price and the enquiry is quoted manually.

Every figure on this page is ex-GST for the lab-ready enclosure, with the 18 per cent GST-inclusive figure shown alongside so that nobody has to compute it. What the figure does not include is the exclusion list above. If you are comparing this page against another supplier's number, compare the exclusion lists first, because a laboratory quotation that does not mention fume hoods, gases, RO water, validation or accreditation has almost certainly left them out too, without saying so.

If the requirement is an office rather than a laboratory, the same platform is priced differently and more cheaply on the Readymade Office Cabin and Prefabricated Office Cabins pages. Ordering a laboratory finish for a room that will hold desks is money spent on a floor junction nobody will ever wash.

Water, drainage, power and the one sealed sleeve

Services are the part of a portable laboratory that goes wrong most often, and the discipline on this page is deliberately strict. One sink or service interface is shown per size, at a coordinate printed on the GA board. That interface is the point at which the unit meets site water and site drainage. The site side of it, the supply, the run, the fall and the disposal, is coordinated separately and is not in the price.

Penetrations through the envelope are controlled the same way. One sealed utility sleeve is provided, and its position is frozen on the drawing before fabrication. Nothing is field-cut. If a future exhaust or an additional utility route is likely, it has to be declared and frozen at the same time, because a hole cut later through a coved and lined enclosure is a hole cut through the reason the enclosure exists. The drawing holds a cowl position as a placeholder; the exhaust itself is excluded until it is quoted.

Electrically, the unit is wired in concealed copper with a distribution board, isolation, RCCB and MCB protection, earthing and circuit segregation as scheduled. Typical starting conductors are 1.5 sq mm for lighting, 2.5 sq mm for sockets and 4 sq mm for higher-load points. Those are starting points, not the design: the final load schedule, protection, derating and voltage drop follow the approved electrical drawing, and the connection to the incoming supply is licensed work carried out on site.

Before dispatch the unit is checked for dimensions, welds, coating, drainage, weather seals, opening operation and electrical continuity. Pressure and leak, polarity, continuity, insulation, earthing and RCCB trip checks on the completed installation are project-specific and belong to the site works.

When a portable mobile laboratory is the wrong choice

It is the wrong choice when the testing has to travel. A room that is lifted by crane onto supports is not a substitute for a vehicle, and treating it as one produces a unit that gets moved twice, damaged once and then abandoned in a corner of the yard.

It is the wrong choice when the room is really an office with a bench in it. If the daily work is paperwork, approvals, drawings and meetings, with occasional sample handling, an office cabin costs less and suits better. The Readymade Office Cabin page carries that specification.

It is the wrong choice when the process needs containment. If the work requires a fume hood, a biosafety cabinet, a gas manifold or a controlled-atmosphere enclosure, the room on this page is the shell those things would sit inside, and every one of them is excluded here. Bring the containment specification first and the enclosure will be quoted around it, rather than the other way round.

And it is the wrong choice when the requirement includes a toilet inside the same unit. A wet zone needs its own waste connection and a different lining, and putting one inside a laboratory shell compromises both. Where a toilet has to be part of the same order, the Porta Cabin with Toilet is the product for it, standing alongside the laboratory rather than inside it.

Frequently asked questions

What is a mobile laboratory?

A laboratory that can be relocated rather than built in place. In India the phrase usually means a lab built into a vehicle. SAMAN's is the other kind: a factory-finished steel room that is delivered, lifted onto supports and used where it stands.

What is a mobile testing laboratory?

A relocatable room set up to run tests next to the process that produces the samples, instead of sending samples away. SAMAN supplies the lab-ready enclosure, benching and sink interface; the test instruments and any accreditation are separate.

What is a portable lab?

On this page it is a steel room finished to laboratory standard before dispatch: coved vinyl floor, washable liner, scheduled bench runs, one sink interface and one sealed utility sleeve, in six sizes from 100 to 400 sq ft.

What are the three types of laboratories?

There is no single official list. In Indian site and factory practice the three that come up are materials and quality-control labs, research labs and clinical or diagnostic labs. The enclosure on this page suits the first, and is used for sample handling and routine testing.

How much does a portable mobile laboratory cost in India?

From ₹2,30,000 ex-GST for the 10×10 ft, 100 sq ft unit, up to ₹7,60,000 ex-GST for the 40×10 ft, 400 sq ft unit. Prices are for the lab-ready enclosure and its scheduled fixed provisions, not for instruments.

Is the sink connected, and is water included?

One sink or service interface is provided per size at the coordinate shown on the GA board. Site water supply, drainage and the connection work are coordinated separately and are not in the page price. RO and DI water plant is excluded.

Can a fume hood be fitted?

Not within this price. Fume hoods, gases, process exhaust, RO or DI water, validation, accreditation and waste treatment are all excluded unless separately quoted. Tell us the containment requirement first and the enclosure is quoted around it.

How is the laboratory lifted and placed?

By four compact top-corner lifting eyes, as one rigid box, onto prepared supports. Lifting connections and capacity for the order are governed by project engineering and the approved shop drawings, not by this page.
